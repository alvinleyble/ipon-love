# Off-app recurring reminders ship as an opt-in periodic WorkManager sweep, daytime-gated, recurring-only

## Context

v1.7.1 Item 12, grilled 2026-07-28. [ADR-0052](0052-recurring-due-date-reminder-foreground.md) shipped the recurring due-date reminder as a **piggyback**: `RecurringReminderWorker` never runs on its own schedule — it runs only when something else has already woken the app. Alvin, after confirming that behaviour on-device, asked for an **opt-in** option so a reminder can also fire without opening the app, with the current foreground/sync-triggered behaviour staying the default. ADR-0052's own Consequences section anticipated exactly this ask.

Three codebase facts, established by inspection at the grill, reshaped the decision before it was made:

- **ADR-0052's ADR-0012 objection was overstated.** ADR-0052's Context argued that off-app scheduling "cuts against ADR-0012's foreground-only posture". But [ADR-0012](0012-interactive-in-process-background-workmanager.md)'s own decision text assigns WorkManager "the deferrable/reliable paths: retry-with-backoff…, the network-reconnect trigger, and **any periodic sweep** — leveraging WorkManager for *survival*…, not immediacy." A `PeriodicWorkRequest` is therefore explicitly *within* ADR-0012's scope. What ADR-0012 forbids is routing *interactive* work through WorkManager; what v1.6.6 Item 36 declined was periodic background **sync** (a product call about network chatter), not periodic work as a mechanism. ADR-0052 evaluated `AlarmManager` and rejected it correctly — it never evaluated a periodic worker at all.

- **The reminder check needs zero network.** `ObservePendingConfirmationsUseCase` derives pending occurrences purely from local Room (rules × categories × already-materialized ids) and stores nothing. So off-app delivery does not require a sync first, needs no `CONNECTED` constraint, and works in airplane mode.

- **The notification workers are cold-launch triggered, not app-open triggered.** `BudgetAlertWorker`, `RecurringReminderWorker`, and `PartnerDebtNotificationWorker` are enqueued from exactly two places: `MainActivity`'s `LaunchedEffect(current.userId)` (once per process launch) and the tail of a completed `SyncWorker` (itself enqueued only at login and by the widget's ⟳). The `onStart` observer syncs in-process but enqueues no workers. So ADR-0052's "app-open" reach is looser than its wording suggests.

The device caveat from ADR-0052 stands unchanged and applies to *every* mechanism considered here: on a force-stopping OEM ROM (Alvin's Transsion/MTK, memory `rom-force-stop-nav-reset`), a force-stopped app receives neither alarms nor workers until it is next opened manually.

## Decision

1. **Off-app delivery is a `PeriodicWorkRequest` targeting the existing `RecurringReminderWorker`.** No `AlarmManager`, no `RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED`, no new manifest permission, no new worker class. **ADR-0052 decision 1's rejection of `AlarmManager` stands and is reaffirmed** on strengthened grounds: inexact alarms (`setAndAllowWhileIdle`) lose their entire schedule on reboot and would need a boot receiver plus reschedule-on-every-rule-change; exact alarms (`setExactAndAllowWhileIdle`) additionally require `USE_EXACT_ALARM` — Play-restricted to alarm clocks, calendars and timers, a rejection risk for a budgeting app — or `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` with a user-granted special permission, to buy minute-precision that a "did you pay rent?" nudge has no use for. WorkManager persists its own schedule and re-arms across reboot for free, and needs no reschedule on rule changes because the worker re-derives the pending list from scratch on every run.

2. **Six-hour period, no flex, no constraints.** A 24-hour period is unsafe in combination with decision 3: WorkManager chooses the moment within each period, and if the OS settles on running it while the device is idle overnight, the daytime gate suppresses it and the next attempt is 24 hours later at a similar hour — the reminder could be suppressed for days while looking healthy. At six hours the runs are six hours apart regardless of phase, so at least two always land inside a thirteen-hour window; the gate can never starve. No `flexInterval` (it would narrow when in each period the OS may run, fighting our own gate). No `CONNECTED` constraint (nothing here touches the network) and no `BatteryNotLow` constraint (a Room read is not worth suppressing, and suppressing it silently is the invisible unreliability this feature exists to reduce). Cost, stated honestly, is up to four *process* wake-ups a day: when the process is cold each one runs the whole of `IponApp.onCreate()` — couple live-sync collectors started, `filesDir/receipts` orphan sweep, temp-export sweep — not merely the Room read. All of it is idempotent and cheap, and it is still less than the balance widget's long-shipped 30-minute `updatePeriodMillis` tick, but the footprint is an app start, not a query. "Up to" because App Standby throttling routinely delivers fewer (see Consequences).

3. **Firing is gated to a fixed daytime delivery window, 08:00–21:00 device-local, half-open `[08:00, 21:00)`.** Self-triggering introduces a failure mode that cannot exist today: deferred jobs are flushed in Doze maintenance windows, so *"Have you paid for your Rent?"* could buzz at 03:40. Outside the window the run does nothing and the next run picks it up. The window is **fixed, not user-configurable** — a time-range picker is a disproportionate surface for a preference nobody opens, and offering hour controls implies a precision `PeriodicWorkRequest` does not have.

4. **The gate applies to the periodic path only, via a `workDataOf` input flag.** `RecurringReminderWorker` is shared with the foreground enqueues in `MainActivity`/`SyncWorker`. A blanket quiet-hours check inside `doWork()` would also stop a 3am *app-open* from surfacing a due reminder — a silent regression in ADR-0052's shipped behaviour. Only the periodic enqueue passes the flag, and only that path honours the window.

5. **Outside the window, suppress the whole check — not just the push.** Writing the inbox row at 03:40 and holding back only the OS push looks tempting, but `recordNotification()` returning `created` is by design the **sole** trigger for the push (ADR-0053): a row written at 03:40 means the 09:00 run finds the slot filled and never pushes, yielding a silent inbox row and no nudge — the opposite of the feature's purpose. Splitting them would need a "pushed yet?" flag on the notification row. The worker therefore returns early, leaving state clean for the next run.

6. **Scope is the recurring due-date reminder only.** `BudgetAlertWorker` and `PartnerDebtNotificationWorker` keep their existing triggers untouched. The recurring reminder is the only notification category whose input is **the passage of time** — an occurrence comes due at midnight whether or not anything happened. Every other category's input is data arriving: a partner-debt alert needs the partner's row to arrive via sync; a personal budget alert needs transactions the user can only enter while in the app; a shared budget alert needs the partner's spend via sync. Since sync is foreground-only (ADR-0012), waking for those would burn wake-ups re-reading data that provably cannot have changed. Making them off-app-aware requires periodic background *sync*, which v1.6.6 Item 36 declined and which would be its own, much larger item.

7. **Opt-in, default OFF, nested under the existing "Recurring reminders" master row.** This is the first thing in the app that makes it wake itself on a schedule; that should be a choice, not something that appears in an update, and when off the periodic work simply does not exist rather than existing-and-suppressed. The sub-row **greys out rather than hides** when the master is off (the shape ADR-0054 decision 9 already established for the Budgets sliders), preserving its stored value so re-enabling the master resumes it. The master row also gains a `SettingsSectionHeader("Recurring")` of its own: it currently sits header-less between the "Budgets" and "Couple" sections, so it reads as a Budgets control, and hanging a sub-row off a mis-grouped row compounds the error. Copy is **hedged, not silent**: it sets the expectation of "a few times a day" rather than a clock time, and admits that some phones limit background activity — without linking to a battery-optimisation fix that v1.6.6 Item 38 proved does not work on the target ROM.

8. **Lifecycle: armed on toggle-on, re-armed at login with `KEEP`, cancelled on toggle-off, sign-out, and delete-account.** A periodic request outlives the app, the process and the session — it survives in WorkManager's own database until something cancels it. The unique work name must be **distinct** from Item 1's one-time `ipon_recurring_reminders`, or a `REPLACE` one-time enqueue on each app open would destroy the schedule. `KEEP` makes the login re-arm a free self-heal that never resets the timer. The sign-out/delete-account cancels sit beside the existing `SyncWorker.WORK_NAME` cancels in `AuthViewModel`/`ProfileViewModel`; without them a signed-out phone wakes every six hours forever. Note what the cancel does **not** buy: `LocalDataWiper` does not clear the notification-preferences store (`@FinanceDataStore`), so the toggle — like every other notification preference already shipped — is **device-global, not per-account**. A different account signing in re-arms the schedule from the inherited `true`, and the cancel only covers the signed-out gap. That is consistent with the existing budget thresholds, per-budget mutes and partner-debt toggle, and making this one preference the sole per-account exception was rejected; scoping all notification preferences per-account is a separate item touching v1.7.1 Items 2-4 and 9. The worker additionally **early-returns on no session** — today it has no such guard and does not need one (it is only enqueued from inside the authenticated composition), but a durable schedule breaks that assumption, and `CurrentUserProvider.userId()` throws by design.

9. **A screen-level notification-permission banner ships with this item.** `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` is requested unconditionally in `MainActivity.onCreate`, and Android shows that dialog at most twice before it becomes a permanent silent no-op; there is no denied-state banner anywhere today (one was designed in the v1.6.6 Item 7 grill and never built). With notifications denied, `notify()` silently does nothing while the switch reads "on" — tolerable for the existing categories (the user finds the alert next time they open the app, which they were doing anyway), but **this feature exists only to reach a user who is not opening the app**, so it becomes a permanent no-op with no signal, indistinguishable on Alvin's device from the force-stop problem. A banner at the top of Settings → Notifications when `NotificationManagerCompat.areNotificationsEnabled()` is false, tapping through to system notification settings, covers all categories. App-level check only — per-channel importance doubles the surface for a rare state.

10. **The only tier-1-tested logic is the delivery-window predicate.** Everything else is orchestration (a second enqueue of an existing worker, four teardown call sites, a banner), matching the no-unit-test precedent for the sibling toggle slices (v1.6.6 Items 7/15/25/34, ADR-0052's own toggle). The gate is extracted as a pure `isWithinDeliveryWindow(now: LocalTime): Boolean` with an injected time, covered by five boundary cases (07:59 suppressed, 08:00 fires, midday fires, 20:59 fires, 21:00 suppressed).

11. **No schema, no Room migration, no sync change.** One local DataStore boolean in `NotificationPreferencesRepository` (mirroring `recurringRemindersEnabled`, itself mirroring `budgetAlertsEnabled`). Fully personal and per-device — no couples-governance question, ADR-0009's push/pull order unaffected.

## Consequences

- **[ADR-0052](0052-recurring-due-date-reminder-foreground.md) is amended, not superseded.** Its decision 1 (no `AlarmManager`) stands; its Consequences instruction that "a future agent should not 'helpfully' add `AlarmManager` scheduling here" also stands, and this ADR is not a licence to revisit it. What changes is that off-app reach is no longer "explicitly out of scope" — it exists as an opt-in periodic sweep. ADR-0052's Context claim that off-app scheduling cuts against ADR-0012 is **corrected here**, so a future reader does not re-reject a legitimate mechanism on a misreading of ADR-0012.

- **The app now has periodic background work for the first time.** Every prior WorkManager use is one-time; the only recurring wake-up was the balance widget's OS-driven `updatePeriodMillis`. This is a deliberate, bounded first — one opt-in worker, one category, no network, daytime-gated — and is **not** a precedent for periodic background *sync*, which stays declined (v1.6.6 Item 36) and would reopen the ADR-0012 question this ADR carefully did not reopen.

- **Delivery remains best-effort and the UI says so.** On force-stopping ROMs the feature delivers nothing until the app is next opened. The hedged copy is load-bearing: it is the only thing preventing a user from filing this as a bug against a working implementation.

- **App Standby buckets throttle this hardest for exactly the user it targets.** Android demotes rarely-opened apps to the `rare`/`restricted` standby buckets, where periodic jobs are deferred to roughly once a day or less. The user this feature exists for — the one who is *not* opening the app — is precisely the one whose app gets demoted, so the six-hour period is a **ceiling, not a promise**. This does not change the decision (it is a further argument for six hours over twenty-four, and for adding no constraints that could suppress a rare permitted run), but it means delivery frequency degrades silently with disuse, and it is the second reason — alongside the force-stop ROM caveat — that decision 7's "a few times a day" copy must never harden into a clock-time promise.

- **The schedule stays armed for users with zero recurring rules.** Auto-cancelling when no rules exist would require re-arming on every rule create; the run costs nothing, so it is left alone.

- **The delivery window reads device-local time with no timezone-travel handling.** A user crossing timezones mid-day may see one run land differently; the window is thirteen hours wide, so this is not observable in practice.

- **Rejected alternatives:** `AlarmManager.setAndAllowWhileIdle` and `setExactAndAllowWhileIdle` (decision 1); a 24-hour period (decision 2); a user-configurable delivery window (decision 3); a blanket quiet-hours check inside the shared worker (decision 4); inbox-row-now/push-later (decision 5); generalising the periodic sweep to budget and partner-debt alerts (decision 6); shipping it default ON, and silent copy that does not admit the OEM limitation (decision 7); relying on the worker's no-session guard instead of cancelling on sign-out (decision 8); a permission warning scoped to this row alone, or none at all (decision 9).
