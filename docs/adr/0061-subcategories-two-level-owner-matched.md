# Subcategories are a two-level, owner-matched hierarchy that degrades by promotion

> **STATUS: DESIGNED, NOT ADOPTED — the feature was deferred on 2026-07-29, the same day this ADR was written.** The design below is complete and build-ready; it is shelved because the *cost it revealed* outweighed the demand behind it, not because any decision here is unresolved. In short: the app's own free cap (10 categories) means the clutter problem this solves barely occurs; gating it to premium (forced by those caps) inverts who gets the fix; the Records filter's multi-select already covers much of the analytical need; and decision 7 below would tax the future web client permanently. See [deferred.md Item 4](../build/deferred.md#item-4--subcategories-parentchild-category-hierarchy) for the un-defer trigger — a beta tester actually hitting the flat-category ceiling. **Nothing in this ADR is in force today.** Its clauses take effect only if the item is un-deferred; in particular **decision 7 is deliberately excluded from the contract freeze** ([v1.7.4.md Item 1](../build/v1.7.4.md#item-1--freeze-the-cross-platform-contract-from-web-phase-0-w2), formerly v1.7.3 Item 1).

**Context.** Categories are flat (`categories` has no parent column). A user who wants detail must either keep categories broad and lose it, or split everything into top-level rows until the picker and the Analysis donut are cluttered with thin slices. A 2026-07-29 competitive gap analysis against the Tarsi budget tracker surfaced nesting as the one real structural hole in an otherwise-equivalent feature set. The design is constrained by three existing decisions that a naive `parent_id` would violate: categories are **exclusively owned** — personal (`user_id`) or couple-owned (`couple_id`), never both (ADR-0018); un-share and `unpair()` both **revert-to-creator** (`user_id = created_by, couple_id = null`), splitting couple rows by their individual creators; and the ADR-0009 FK push order sequences **tables**, never rows *within* a table.

**Decision.**

**1. Exactly two levels, enforced in the database.** A row with a `parent_id` may not itself be a parent. Rollup is therefore always a one-level sum — no recursive CTE, no Kotlin tree-walk, no cycle guard. (Room cannot return a recursive CTE as a `Flow` across levels anyway.)

**2. A parent stays selectable.** "Food" remains an ordinary pickable category holding its own transactions; children are purely additive. No existing transaction is ever orphaned, and a user who ignores the feature sees no change. Rejected: parent-as-header-only — it strands every transaction already filed on a category the moment it gains its first child, and adds a second step to the app's most frequent action.

**3. Same-owner invariant.** A child's owner must always equal its parent's — both personal-to-the-same-user, or both couple-owned. Enforced in the schema, the un-share usecase, and the `unpair()` RPC.

**4. Degrade by promotion, never by destruction.** Whenever an operation would break the invariant or strand a child, the child is **promoted to top-level** (`parent_id = null`) rather than tombstoned or left dangling:
- **Un-share** a parent: children you created follow it to personal; children the partner created are promoted (staying couple-owned).
- **`unpair()`**: same-creator children follow their parent; cross-creator children are promoted. This is what keeps unpair **unilateral and always-succeeds** (ADR-0008) — without it, a parent created by A and a child created by B revert to different owners, and B's client (which purges A's rows) is left holding a child whose `parent_id` references a row it does not have.
- **Delete** a parent (soft, ADR-0010): the parent is tombstoned and its children are promoted. Deletion is permanent, so no action on a parent may destroy a child.
- **Archive** a parent: children cascade instead. Archiving is reversible and lossless, so the hierarchy is preserved and restored intact.

**5. A parent's budget and chart slice roll up its children.** `BudgetProgressCalculator.spent()` and `AnalysisCalculator` match against `{id} ∪ children(id)` instead of an exact id. A child may also carry its own budget, and the same peso counts toward both — this is not new: an overall budget (`category_id = null`) already counts categories that have their own budgets. Rollover chains stay exact-match, so no scope-crossing chain can form. Analysis shows parent-level slices only, with the child breakdown behind a list-row expansion (reusing the screen's existing expand idiom rather than adding canvas hit-testing).

**6. `exclude_from_analysis` inherits by OR** (ADR-0049). A transaction is excluded if its own category **or** its parent carries the flag: a flagged parent covers everything beneath it, while one child under an unflagged parent may still be flagged on its own. Without this the parent's rollup slice would disagree with the children inside it.

**7. `parent_id` is a real foreign key, and the categories push is ordered parents-first.** The dirty set is sorted so parents precede children before chunking, which holds across batch boundaries because parents sort globally first. **This is an intra-table ordering rule, which ADR-0009 §3 does not cover** — it therefore extends the cross-platform contract (`docs/web/cross-platform-contract.md` §3), and a second client that ignores it will fail the FK and wedge the batch, the same poison-row failure the ADR-0018 amendment describes. Room keeps no `@ForeignKey` and tolerates a transient dangling parent on pull, as it does today. Soft deletes make this purely an insert-ordering window: tombstones are permanent (ADR-0010), so a parent row never actually disappears.

**8. A child's `type` must equal its parent's**, enforced in the schema. Otherwise an INCOME child would sum into an EXPENSE parent's rollup.

**9. Re-parenting is allowed; demoting a row that has children is not.** The category editor gains an optional parent selector, so existing flat categories can be reorganised in place — transactions are untouched, keeping the same `category_id` as it moves in the tree. This is the only adoption path for existing data. Moving a row that already has children is blocked, since it would create a third level.

**10. Nesting is a premium capability** (`SUBCATEGORIES` soft gate, shipped **dormant** per the standing build stance). Free keeps flat categories; child rows are ordinary categories counting toward the existing `maxPersonalCategories`/`maxSharedCategories` caps, so **no new `PlanLimits` field** is added — avoiding the §11 finding-#3 hazard that `PlanLimits` and `PlanLimitsOverrides` have no compile-time link. Recording is still never gated: every peso remains filable on free.

**Consequences.** The hierarchy survives every lifecycle event the couple layer can throw at it without a dangling reference, a wedged push, or a destroyed row, and `unpair()` keeps its unilateral guarantee. The costs are explicit: a parent budget and a child budget genuinely double-count (accepted — it mirrors overall-vs-category, and per-budget mute already exists for the duplicate alerts, ADR-0054); a promoted child can surprise a user by appearing at top level after a delete or unpair; and decision 7 adds a clause the web client must implement, so **the contract freeze ([v1.7.4.md Item 1](../build/v1.7.4.md#item-1--freeze-the-cross-platform-contract-from-web-phase-0-w2), formerly v1.7.3 Item 1) must record it**. The starter catalog stays flat deliberately — seeding children would mint new deterministic v5 UUIDs that the same contract freeze (§1) would then have to enumerate.

**Rejected.** Arbitrary nesting (recursive rollup, cycle prevention, no Room support); personal-only subcategories (excludes the shared case, arguably the main one for a couples app); free mixing of parent/child ownership (multiplies ADR-0005 redaction/purge cases and the paths to a dangling reference); cascading deletes (worst data-loss-per-tap in the app, and contrary to the preservation stance settled in v1.6.7 Item 5); blocking archive/delete until children are handled (dead-ends the user and has no precedent — unpair is deliberately never blocked); a flat picker grid or a two-step drill-down (the first reintroduces the clutter this feature removes, the second makes tapping a parent mean "open" rather than "choose", contradicting decision 2); and a dedicated subcategory cap field (four unlinked edit sites for a limit the existing caps already express).
